## Strategic Context

The GCC roadside assistance market is large, fragmented, and ripe for marketplace digitization. Incumbents fall into three buckets:

1. **Insurer-affiliated dispatch** — trusted but slow, opaque, and single-provider assigned
2. **Single-brand service apps** — narrow category coverage (tow-only or battery-only)
3. **Informal networks** — fast sometimes, but unverified, unpriced, and unaccountable

Salik Assist enters as a **multi-category marketplace** with **transparent top-3 competitive offers**, asset-light operations, and GCC-native trust standards. The strategic bet: win Dubai density first, prove unit economics, then expand emirates and KSA with B2B channels accelerating demand.

## Strategic Positioning

**Category:** Roadside assistance marketplace  
**For:** Motorists, fleets, and insurers needing fast, verified roadside help  
**Unlike:** Insurer call centers and informal tow networks  
**Salik Assist:** Surfaces three competitive offers in minutes with tracked arrival and guaranteed payment

**Positioning statement:**  
*When your car stops in the UAE, Salik Assist gets you three trusted rescue options—not one unknown truck an hour away.*

## Strategic Pillars

### Pillar 1: Liquidity Before Features

No feature matters if customers receive zero or one offer. Strategy prioritizes provider density and zone-level liquidity over category expansion or premium features until Dubai core zones hit liquidity targets (≥65% of requests with 3+ offers within 5 minutes).

### Pillar 2: Trust as Product

Verification, ratings, dispute resolution, and SLA adherence are not back-office—they are customer-facing product surfaces. Unverified supply never appears in top-3 results.

### Pillar 3: Choice Drives Conversion

The top-3 provider model is strategic, not cosmetic. It differentiates from insurer auto-assign and builds customer habit through agency and price comparison.

### Pillar 4: B2B Accelerates B2C

Insurance and fleet partnerships provide predictable demand that attracts providers. Strategy sequences one anchor B2B pilot alongside consumer launch—not B2B-only or B2C-only.

### Pillar 5: Asset-Light Scale

Salik Assist does not buy trucks. Capital deploys to supply acquisition, trust systems, and demand generation—enabling faster geographic expansion than vertically integrated competitors.

## Winning in Dubai First

Dubai is the strategic beachhead, not merely a pilot city.

| Factor | Why Dubai |
|--------|-----------|
| Vehicle density | High breakdown frequency per km² |
| Digital adoption | Card/wallet payment norm; app-comfortable population |
| Provider concentration | Al Quoz, Jebel Ali, industrial zones with tow/mechanic clusters |
| Manageable geography | Zone-based liquidity achievable with 150–200 providers |
| Brand adjacency | Salik name recognition in UAE (with positioning care) |
| Regulatory clarity | Marketplace model viable with proper provider liability framing |

**Dubai launch zones (priority):** Sheikh Zayed Road corridor, Dubai Marina/JBR, Downtown/Business Bay, Dubai Mall corridor, E611/E44 highway segments, Al Quoz industrial.

## Business Examples

**Example 1 — Liquidity-first strategy in action:** Marina zone shows Liquidity Index of 52% (below 65% target). Strategy pauses Instagram ads in Marina and redirects field sales to recruit 15 battery providers within 10 km. Category expansion to mobile mechanic deferred until tow/battery liquidity recovers.

**Example 2 — Competitive response:** A single-service tow app launches aggressive pricing in Dubai. Salik Assist does not match price war; instead emphasizes top-3 choice, battery+tow bundle categories, and insurer co-marketing. SCAJ grows 18% month-over-month through differentiation, not subsidy.

**Example 3 — B2B without disintermediation:** Insurer pilot routes 200 jobs/month with pre-negotiated caps. Customers still see three in-network providers and choose based on ETA. Insurer gains cost predictability; customer retains agency—strategy pillar intact.

## Business Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Dubai-only until liquidity gates met | Prevents national launch with hollow experience |
| Top-3 offers over auto-assign | Strategic differentiation and trust |
| Tow + battery anchors before tire/fuel/mechanic | Highest frequency + clearest provider supply |
| Marketplace-only; no fleet acquisition | Capital efficiency and provider alignment |
| One B2B anchor at launch vs. many shallow pilots | Depth beats breadth for credibility |
| Commission-primary revenue with optional provider subscriptions | Aligns incentives with SCAJ |
| Compete with incumbents on experience, not subsidies | Sustainable unit economics |

## Risks

| Risk | Strategic Impact | Mitigation |
|------|----------------|------------|
| Insurer builds exclusive network | Blocks B2B pillar | Early partnership; prove superior SLA |
| Provider multi-homing without preference | Weak loyalty | Earnings tools, payment speed, demand volume |
| Super-app bundles roadside free | Commoditization | Specialization, verification, B2B depth |
| Dubai liquidity never reaches 65% | Top-3 promise fails | Zone shrink, supply incentives, category focus |
| Regulatory reclassification | Business model threat | Legal counsel; marketplace terms |

## Assumptions

- Dubai can sustain 85,000+ SCAJ in Year 1 with focused execution
- 150–200 verified providers sufficient for launch zone coverage
- Top-3 model legally and operationally viable under UAE consumer protection norms
- At least one insurer willing to pilot within 6 months of launch
- Customer willingness to pay platform-mediated prices vs. cash informal market
