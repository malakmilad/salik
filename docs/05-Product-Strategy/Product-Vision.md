## Product Vision Statement

**Salik Assist will be the product motorists open first when their vehicle stops—delivering three trusted offers within minutes, transparent pricing, live arrival tracking, and guaranteed payment resolution, starting in Dubai and scaling across the GCC.**

The product exists to transform roadside emergencies from opaque, stressful phone calls into a confident, choice-driven rescue experience—while giving independent providers a professional demand channel that respects their autonomy.

---

## The Problem We Solve for Customers

Motorists in the UAE and broader GCC experience breakdowns in contexts where every minute matters:

- **Uncertainty:** Unknown tow numbers, unclear pricing, and no visibility into who is coming
- **Delay:** Insurer call centers and informal networks often take 20–45 minutes before dispatch
- **Trust deficit:** Unverified operators, language barriers, and fear of overcharging at vulnerable moments
- **Fragmentation:** Different contacts for tow, battery, tire, fuel, and mobile mechanic needs

The product must collapse this fragmentation into a single, reliable flow optimized for stress, safety, and speed.

---

## The Problem We Solve for Providers

Independent roadside professionals face the opposite challenge:

- **Idle capacity:** Trucks and technicians sit unused between phone referrals
- **Payment risk:** Cash disputes, slow insurer reimbursements, and no-shows
- **No reputation layer:** Quality operators cannot differentiate from unreliable competitors
- **Digital exclusion:** No access to structured demand from insurers, fleets, or app-native customers

The product must deliver qualified jobs, fair competition, and payment certainty without turning providers into employees.

---

## Product Promise

| Audience | Promise |
|----------|---------|
| **Customers** | Help on the road in minutes—not hours—with choice, clarity, and accountability |
| **Providers** | More paid jobs, less admin, and a reputation that compounds |
| **Partners (insurers, fleets)** | Auditable dispatch, SLA-backed fulfillment, and predictable costs |
| **Salik Assist** | A marketplace where value is created only when jobs complete successfully |

## Core Product Experience

The end-to-end product journey is designed around five moments:

### 1. Request (Under 60 Seconds)

Customer shares location, vehicle type, and issue category. The product minimizes typing during distress—smart defaults, map pin, and category shortcuts for common UAE scenarios (flat tire on Sheikh Zayed Road, dead battery in mall parking, accident tow).

### 2. Offers (Top 3 Within Minutes)

The product surfaces up to three matched provider offers showing ETA, estimated price range, star rating, verification badges, and service scope. Customer chooses—not the platform auto-assigning silently.

### 3. Track (Real-Time Confidence)

From acceptance to arrival, customer sees provider identity, live ETA updates, and in-app communication. Anxiety reduction is a product feature, not a marketing claim.

### 4. Complete (Fair Settlement)

Service completion triggers transparent payment—card, wallet, or B2B settlement. Receipt, job summary, and warranty/dispute path are immediate.

### 5. Rate (Trust Flywheel)

Post-job rating and optional tip reinforce provider quality signals that power future matching. Low-rated or non-compliant providers lose visibility.

---

## Top-3 Provider Model as Product Differentiator

Auto-assign models optimize for platform efficiency; the top-3 model optimizes for **customer agency at the moment of highest stress**.

| Dimension | Auto-Assign | Top-3 Offers (Salik Assist) |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------------|
| Customer perception | "Someone is coming" (opaque) | "I chose the best option" (empowered) |
| Price transparency | Often revealed late | Compared upfront |
| Provider motivation | Race to accept first | Compete on ETA, price, reputation |
| Trust | Platform vouches blindly | Customer sees credentials side-by-side |
| Marketplace health | Winner-take-all dynamics | Multiple providers stay engaged |

The product vision treats competitive offers as non-negotiable for launch—not a future enhancement.

## Product Horizons

### Horizon 1 — Launch (Months 0–12): Dubai Rescue Core

- Consumer app (Arabic/English) for tow and battery as anchor categories
- Provider app for offer response, navigation, and job completion
- Top-3 matching in defined Dubai zones and highway corridors
- Verification badges, ratings, and basic dispute resolution
- Target: prove SCAJ density in Dubai before emirate expansion

### Horizon 2 — Regional Depth (Months 12–24): UAE + Categories

- Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, and inter-emirate highway coverage
- Tire, fuel delivery, mobile mechanic categories
- B2B partner portals for insurers and fleets
- Subscription tiers for providers; loyalty for repeat customers

### Horizon 3 — GCC Scale (Months 24–36): Infrastructure Layer

- KSA entry (Riyadh, Jeddah)
- Enterprise SLAs, branded partner experiences
- EV rescue, desert recovery, luxury vehicle specialists
- Salik Assist as default dispatch layer for mobility partners

## Success Criteria for the Product

The product vision is achieved when:

1. **Motorists in Dubai** name Salik Assist unprompted when discussing breakdowns
2. **Median time to three offers** is under 5 minutes in launch zones
3. **Successfully Completed Assisted Jobs** grow month-over-month with improving repeat rates
4. **Providers** report higher earnings and prefer Salik Assist demand over phone-only dispatch
5. **Partners** route material job volume through the product because SLAs and audit trails outperform alternatives

---

## What the Product Will Not Do

- Operate owned tow fleets or employ providers on W2/labor contracts
- Hide pricing until after service without customer consent
- List unverified providers in customer-facing results
- Expand into unrelated home services (plumbing, cleaning, etc.)
- Prioritize download vanity metrics over completed job quality

## Business Examples

**Example 1 — Mall parking battery failure:** A customer in Dubai Mall parking selects "Dead Battery." Within 2 minutes, three verified battery specialists offer ETAs of 15, 19, and 28 minutes with price ranges of AED 150–180, AED 140–170, and AED 130–160. Customer picks the 15-minute provider with 4.9 stars. Job completes; SCAJ recorded.

**Example 2 — Highway tow with choice:** A motorist on E611 requests accident tow. Three providers respond; customer rejects the cheapest due to low recent rating and selects mid-priced operator with flatbed specialization. Product vision fulfilled: choice + trust over race-to-bottom pricing.

**Example 3 — Provider product value:** A tow operator in Al Quoz receives 8 push notifications daily, accepts 3, completes 2 with guaranteed card payment. Provider dashboard shows weekly earnings up 30% vs. pre-Salik phone dispatch—validating supply-side product promise.

## Business Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Product vision anchored on completed jobs, not requests | Requests without fulfillment create false progress; aligns with North Star |
| Top-3 offers as launch requirement | Core differentiator vs. insurer auto-dispatch and informal networks |
| Dubai-first product scope | Concentrate liquidity before geographic sprawl dilutes experience |
| Arabic/English parity from day one | Product cannot be "English-first with Arabic later" in UAE market |
| Verification visible in offer cards | Trust is product UI, not back-office policy |
| Customer choice preserved even when B2B pays | Partner jobs still show provider options where contract allows |

## Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Product complexity slows distressed users | High | Ruthless launch scope; ≤5 taps to request |
| Top-3 model fails if supply thin | High | Zone gating; pre-launch provider density targets |
| Provider app adoption lag | High | Field onboarding; SMS fallback for offer response |
| Feature creep before Dubai liquidity | Medium | Horizon gates tied to SCAJ milestones |
| Brand confusion with Salik toll | Medium | Distinct product identity in UX; co-brand testing |

---

## Assumptions

- Dubai motorists will complete app onboarding before or during first breakdown if value proposition is clear
- Three competitive offers materially improve conversion vs. single auto-assigned provider
- Providers will respond to push offers within 2 minutes when properly incentivized
- Tow and battery categories generate sufficient frequency to prove marketplace mechanics
- Post-job ratings create meaningful quality differentiation within 90 days

---

## Open Questions

- Should the product support guest checkout (no account) for first-time emergency users?
- Minimum provider rating threshold to appear in top-3 results at launch?
- How prominently should insurer partnership branding appear in B2B-sourced requests?
- Product naming in-app: "Salik Assist" vs. shortened "Assist" for international clarity?
