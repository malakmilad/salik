## Growth Thesis

Salik Assist grows when **more motorists complete more jobs in more zones with improving unit economics**. Growth is not decoupled from liquidity—the top-3 model only works with sufficient provider density.

**Core growth equation:**

```
SCAJ Growth = (New Requesters × Match Rate × Fill Rate) + (Repeat Requesters × Frequency) + B2B Volume
```

**Strategic growth priorities (in order):**
1. Liquidity (supply density per zone)
2. Conversion (request → accept → complete)
3. Retention (repeat jobs and referrals)
4. Efficient acquisition (CAC discipline)
5. B2B scale (predictable volume)

---

## Growth Model

### Flywheel

```
More providers in zone
    → Faster top-3 offers
        → Higher customer conversion
            → More SCAJ + ratings
                → Provider earnings + reputation
                    → Provider retention + referrals
                        → More providers in zone
```

### Growth Constraints

| Constraint | Growth Response |
|------------|-----------------|
| Low Liquidity Index | Pause paid demand; recruit supply |
| High CAC | Shift to referral and organic |
| Low fill rate | Fix ops/payout before more demand |
| Provider churn | Earnings tools, bonuses, support |
| Zone saturation | Expand geography per expansion gates |

## Supply Growth Levers

Demand growth without supply creates churn. Supply growth levers:

| Lever | Description | Target Impact |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **Zone recruitment sprints** | Field sales blitz in low-liquidity zones | +10 providers/zone/month |
| **Provider referral bonuses** | AED 200 per activated provider | 20% of new supply |
| **Earnings guarantees** | Minimum monthly for qualified new providers | Reduce early churn 15% |
| **Response rate incentives** | Bonus for <2 min median response | +8 pts response rate |
| **Premium tier (future)** | Subscription for priority matching | Provider ARPU +12% |
| **Training and certification** | EV, luxury, desert categories | Enable category expansion |
| **Payout speed** | 48-hour settlement | Retention +10 pts |

**Rule:** For every 1,000 incremental monthly requests in a zone, maintain ≥15 active providers per 10 km² launch zone equivalent.

## Retention and Lifecycle

Roadside assistance is inherently infrequent (~1–2 events/driver/year). Retention strategy focuses on **top-of-mind** and **household coverage**.

### Customer Lifecycle Stages

| Stage | Goal | Tactics |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Acquire** | First download | Paid, referral, B2B |
| **Activate** | First SCAJ | First-job discount; frictionless request |
| **Engage** | Pre-breakdown top-of-mind | Push: summer battery check; highway tips |
| **Retain** | Second job or referral | Referral credit; family account linking |
| **Win-back** | Lapsed 12+ months | Seasonal reactivation offer |

### Retention Programs

| Program | Description | KPI |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| **Family plan** | Link up to 3 vehicles per account | Household MAR |
| **Salik Assist reminder** | Annual "save us in your glove box" card with dealerships | Unaided awareness |
| **Post-job referral prompt** | Ask after 5-star rating | Referral rate |
| **Loyalty credits** | AED 10 credit every 3rd job | Repeat rate |
| **B2B sticky** | Insurer embed = zero-switching cost | B2B retention |

**Target:** 12% 90-day repeat rate Y1; 22% Y3 (includes multi-vehicle household and B2B).

## Referral and Viral Loops

Breakdowns are social—motorists call friends, post on social media, warn colleagues.

### Primary Referral Loop

```
Customer completes job (high relief moment)
    → Prompt: "Give a friend AED 25 rescue credit"
        → Friend downloads before or during their emergency
            → Friend completes first SCAJ
                → Both receive credit
                    → Original customer refers again
```

### Secondary Loops

| Loop | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| **Provider → customer** | Provider shares code for preferred repeat customers |
| **Provider → provider** | Recruitment bonus for supply growth |
| **B2B → customer** | Insurer email: "Your policy includes Salik Assist" |
| **Dealership → customer** | New car delivery includes app setup |

### Referral Guardrails

- Credits apply after referee's **completed** job (anti-fraud)
- Max 10 referrals/month/customer
- No referral prompt before job completion
- Monitor collusion patterns (same device, same location)

**Target:** Referral channel 15% of new requests by M9; 25% by M18.

## B2B Growth Engine

B2B provides **predictable SCAJ** with lower marginal CAC.

### B2B Growth Stages

| Stage | Activity | SCAJ Contribution |
|-------|----------|-------------------|
| **Pilot** | 1 partner, 200 jobs | 5% |
| **Contract** | Annual agreement, SLA | 12% |
| **Embed** | In-app integration, co-brand | 20% |
| **Exclusive corridor** | Preferred network in zones | 30%+ (select partners) |

### B2B Growth Tactics

- Case study from anchor pilot (cost per dispatch, CSAT)
- Industry events (insurance, fleet, logistics UAE)
- Dedicated partnerships sales hire per [Executive Summary](../01-Executive/Executive-Summary.md)
- Volume tier pricing for enterprise
- Quarterly business reviews with SLA scorecards

**Constraint:** B2B growth must not degrade consumer top-3 experience where contract allows choice.

## Category Expansion Growth

New categories drive **frequency** and **ARPU** but risk liquidity dilution.

### Category Rollout Sequence

| Order | Category | Growth Rationale | Gate |

| 1 | Tow | Highest GMV | Launch |
| 2 | Battery | High frequency (summer) | Launch |

| 5 | Fuel delivery | Highway niche | M8 |
| 6 | EV rescue | Future-proofing | Y2 |

**Gate per category:** ≥20 verified specialists in zone + no Liquidity Index regression >5 points in anchor categories.

## Business Examples

**Example 1 — Liquidity-gated spend:** Performance marketing ROAS strong at 4:1, but Marina Liquidity Index drops to 59%. Growth pauses Marina ads, redirects $15K/month to provider recruitment. Index recovers to 66%; ads resume.

**Example 2 — Referral loop:** Customer completes highway tow, rates 5 stars, shares referral with colleague. Colleague's battery dies 3 weeks later—uses code. CAC $0 for second customer. Referral share in corporate district rises to 22%.

**Example 3 — B2B scale:** Insurer pilot 200 jobs → contract 2,000 jobs/month. B2B share rises from 5% to 14% in one quarter. Consumer Liquidity Index stable—B2B jobs concentrated in existing high-liquidity zones.

**Example 4 — Summer battery campaign:** June battery SCAJ 40% above baseline. Growth runs "Summer Battery Ready" push notifications to lapsed users. Battery category SCAJ +25%; provider surge bonus prevents TTFO degradation.

## Business Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Liquidity gates control paid demand | Prevent growth that destroys experience |
| Referral as primary organic engine | Low CAC; high-trust moment post-job |
| B2B as parallel growth track | Predictable volume; insurer trust transfer |
| Category expansion gated on liquidity | Principle 8: Liquidity Over Novelty |
| Contribution margin discipline from M9 | Growth must trend profitable |
| Household/family accounts for retention | Infrequent category needs multi-vehicle reach |

## Risks

| Risk | Growth Impact |
|------|---------------|
| Paid CAC inflation (emergency keywords) | LTV:CAC breach |
| Low repeat rate in infrequent category | Over-reliance on paid acquisition |
| Referral fraud | Credit abuse; metric distortion |
| B2B partner concentration | Single partner churn shocks SCAJ |
| Competitor subsidy war | Margin compression |
| Over-marketing before supply | Liquidity collapse; bad reviews |
