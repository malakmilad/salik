## Expansion Vision

Salik Assist expands **only from a position of proven liquidity**—winning Dubai completely, then the UAE, then the GCC—becoming the default roadside marketplace infrastructure in each market before entering the next.

**2031 aspiration (from [Vision](../01-Executive/Vision.md)):** 12+ countries, 45,000+ providers, 3M+ annual SCAJ run-rate, 50%+ B2B penetration.

**Near-term focus:** Dubai → UAE → KSA. No expansion geography activates without meeting objective phase gates.

---

## Expansion Principles

1. **Depth before breadth** — 65% Liquidity Index in current geography before adding new
2. **Clone, then adapt** — Replicate Dubai playbook; localize only where required
3. **Supply-led entry** — Providers recruited before demand marketing in new market
4. **B2B as beachhead** — Insurer/fleet partnerships accelerate new market credibility
5. **Asset-light everywhere** — No fleet acquisition in new markets
6. **One market owner** — City/country GM accountable for local SCAJ and liquidity

## Phase 1: Dubai Dominance

### Objective

Establish Salik Assist as the leading digital roadside marketplace in Dubai with sustainable liquidity, unit economics, and brand recognition.

### Geographic Scope

- Wave 1–4 Dubai zone activation per [Launch Strategy](./Launch-Strategy.md)
- E11 Dubai–Abu Dhabi connector (commuter corridor)

### Success Criteria (Phase 1 Exit Gate)

| Gate | Threshold |
|------|-----------|
| Monthly SCAJ (Dubai) | ≥10,000 |
| Liquidity Index (Dubai aggregate) | ≥68% |
| Customer NPS | ≥35 |
| Provider 90-day retention | ≥70% |
| Contribution margin per job | Positive |
| Unaided awareness (Dubai drivers) | ≥15% |

### Expansion Actions Within Phase 1

- Activate Mirdif, DSO, Jebel Ali waves
- Add tire, mobile mechanic categories
- Sign 2+ B2B contracts with UAE national coverage clauses (future)

**Do not enter Abu Dhabi city until Phase 1 gate met.**

## Phase 2: UAE National Coverage

### Objective

Extend proven marketplace model to Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, and northern emirates—treating each emirate as a **liquidity island** with its own supply build.

### Emirate Sequencing

| Order | Emirate | Rationale | Earliest Entry |
|-------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | **Abu Dhabi** | Capital, high vehicle density, insurer HQs | Q5 post-launch (M10–12) |
| 2 | **Sharjah** | Dubai commuter overlap; shared corridors | M12–14 |
| 3 | **Ajman / RAK / Fujairah** | Northern corridor | M16–18 |
| 4 | **UAQ** | Smaller; partner-led | M18–20 |

### Per-Emirate Launch Playbook (Clone of Dubai)

1. Map launch zones and highway corridors
2. Recruit 80–120 verified providers (emirate-scaled)
3. Soft launch with 1,000 beta users
4. Achieve Liquidity Index ≥60% in beta zones
5. Public launch with geo-fenced marketing only
6. B2B partner extension (insurer national policy)

### Abu Dhabi Specifics

| Factor | Implication |
|--------|-------------|
| Government and fleet density | B2B-first outreach to government fleet operators |
| Yas Island, Saadiyat tourism | Rental and hospitality partnerships |
| Long highway distances (E11) | Tow category priority; ETA transparency critical |
| Provider clusters | Mussafah, ICAD industrial zones for field sales |

### Phase 2 Exit Gate

| Gate | Threshold |
|------|-----------|
| UAE monthly SCAJ | ≥18,000 |
| Each live emirate Liquidity Index | ≥60% |
| UAE B2B job share | ≥18% |
| Cross-emirate jobs (corridor) | ≥8% of UAE SCAJ |
| UAE provider active count | ≥350 |

## Phase 3: KSA Entry

### Objective

Enter Saudi Arabia—GCC's largest market—with Riyadh and Jeddah as dual beachheads, replicating UAE marketplace model with KSA localization.

### Why KSA After UAE

| Factor | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Market size | 3–4× UAE SAM per [Executive Summary](../01-Executive/Executive-Summary.md) |
| Motorization | High and growing |
| Digital payments | Mature; smartphone penetration strong |
| Complexity | Regulatory, cultural, geographic—requires proven playbook |
| Proof point | UAE SCAJ and B2B case studies de-risk entry |

### KSA City Sequencing

| Order | City | Rationale |
|-------|------|-----------|
| 1 | **Riyadh** | Capital, largest vehicle population |
| 2 | **Jeddah** | Commercial hub, Mecca corridor tourism |
| 3 | **Dammam / Khobar** | Eastern province industrial |
| 4 | **Mecca / Medina** | Seasonal; pilgrimage roadside demand |

### KSA Entry Requirements (Pre-Entry Gate)

| Requirement | Status Needed |
|-------------|---------------|
| UAE Phase 2 gate met | Yes |
| KSA legal entity and marketplace opinion | Complete |
| KSA GM / city manager hired | In seat 3 months pre-launch |
| 200+ KSA providers recruited (Riyadh) | Verified |
| Anchor KSA B2B LOI (insurer or fleet) | Signed |
| Arabic product localization (KSA dialect review) | Complete |
| Funding tranche for KSA allocated | Board approved |

### KSA Localization Highlights

- **Payments:** Mada, local wallets, cashless preference
- **Language:** Arabic primary; formal and Najdi dialect customer support scripts
- **Regulation:** Saudi transport and marketplace compliance
- **Gender / service:** Appropriate provider assignment where requested
- **Highway network:** Riyadh–Dammam, Jeddah–Mecca corridor mapping
- **Brand:** Evaluate "Salik Assist" vs. localized brand given UAE toll association

### KSA Launch Targets (First 12 Months)

| Metric | Target |
|--------|--------|
| Riyadh monthly SCAJ (M12 KSA) | 4,000 |
| Jeddah monthly SCAJ (M12 KSA) | 2,500 |
| KSA Liquidity Index (core zones) | ≥55% Y1 KSA |
| KSA active providers | 400 |
| KSA B2B contracts | 2 |

## Expansion Gates Framework

### Universal Gate Checklist (Any New Geography)

| # | Gate | Owner |
|---|------|-------|
| 1 | Prior geography liquidity gate met | GM |
| 2 | Provider pipeline ≥ zone-scaled target | Operations |
| 3 | Legal/marketplace clearance | Legal |
| 4 | Local GM in seat | HR |
| 5 | Beta SCAJ ≥ geography minimum | Operations |
| 6 | Liquidity Index ≥ 60% beta zones | Operations |
| 7 | B2B anchor LOI or equivalent | Partnerships |
| 8 | Marketing budget allocated | Finance |
| 9 | Support coverage (language, hours) | Support |
| 10 | Exec expansion review approval | CEO |

### Gate Failure Response

- **Delay entry** 4–8 weeks; remediate failing gate
- **Narrow geography** (e.g., Abu Dhabi island only, not full emirate)
- **Never** launch nationally on brand alone without supply

## Partnership-Led Expansion

Partnerships accelerate expansion when entering markets with local credibility gaps.

| Partner Type | Expansion Role |
|--------------|----------------|
| **National insurer** | Instant demand; policyholder trust |
| **Fleet / rental** | B2B volume in new city |
| **Fuel retailer** | QR distribution; provider referrals |
| **Automotive OEM** | Roadside program white-label |
| **Local tow association** | Bulk provider onboarding |
| **Telecom** | Co-marketing, billing integration |

**KSA strategy:** Secure one national insurer or fleet LOI before public Riyadh launch—mirroring UAE anchor playbook.

## Business Examples

**Example 1 — Abu Dhabi gated entry:** Dubai hits Phase 1 gate at M11 (10,200 monthly SCAJ, 69% liquidity). Abu Dhabi field sales begins with 90 providers in Mussafah. Beta 800 users; Liquidity Index 58% week 3. Public launch delayed 4 weeks until 62%. Abu Dhabi monthly SCAJ 1,800 by M3 Abu Dhabi live.

**Example 2 — KSA insurer beachhead:** Saudi insurer signs LOI covering 500K policyholders. Riyadh launch includes co-branded "Roadside Help" in insurer app. Month 1 KSA: 1,200 SCAJ, 45% B2B-sourced. Consumer marketing deferred 6 weeks while B2B proves liquidity.

**Example 3 — Oman corridor:** UAE customer breaks down on E11 toward Muscat. Cross-border agreement allows 2 Oman-verified providers in offer pool. Job completes; SCAJ attributed to UAE with cross-border tag. Informs full Oman Phase 4 entry business case.

**Example 4 — Sharjah commuter overlap:** Sharjah launch leverages 40 Dubai providers with Sharjah licenses—dual-zone activation. Liquidity Index Sharjah 64% at launch vs. 52% if Sharjah-only supply. Model: shared corridor supply where legally permitted.

## Business Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Dubai dominance before Abu Dhabi | Prove playbook; avoid multi-front liquidity drain |
| Per-emirate liquidity gates | Each emirate is distinct supply market |
| KSA after UAE profitability path | Complexity requires proven model |
| City GM model vs. remote management | Local accountability for SCAJ and supply |
| B2B anchor per new country | Credibility and demand floor |
| Asset-light in all markets | Consistent capital efficiency |
| Cross-border corridors before full country | Test demand with lower fixed cost |
| Evaluate KSA brand separately | Salik toll association may not transfer |

## Risks

| Risk | Expansion Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------------|------------|
| Premature KSA entry | Capital burn; brand damage | Strict Phase 3 gates |
| UAE emirate liquidity desert | National brand weakens | Emirate-level gates |
| KSA regulatory block | Market inaccessible | Early legal engagement |
| Provider license reciprocity failure | Cross-border offers illegal | Legal clearance per corridor |
| Local incumbent response | CAC spike in new market | B2B anchor + differentiation |
| Management bandwidth | All expansions slip | One major geography at a time |
| Currency / payment friction | Conversion drop | Local payment partnerships |

## Assumptions

- No GCC-wide competitor achieves liquidity dominance before Salik Assist UAE scale
