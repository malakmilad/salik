## Company Overview

**Salik Assist** is a marketplace platform that connects motorists experiencing vehicle breakdowns or roadside emergencies with nearby, verified service providers. The platform does not employ providers or own fleets; it orchestrates discovery, matching, booking, payment, and quality assurance between independent supply and demand.

**Tagline:** *Help on the road, on demand.*

**Initial geography:** United Arab Emirates (Dubai and Abu Dhabi corridors), expanding to KSA and broader GCC.

**Stage:** Pre-launch / Series A readiness.

---

## Problem & Opportunity

Motorists in the GCC face fragmented, opaque, and slow roadside assistance when breakdowns occur. Current options include:

- Insurer-affiliated call centers with long wait times and limited transparency
- Informal tow-truck networks reached via phone or roadside signage
- Single-brand apps with narrow service coverage
- Whatsapp-based dispatch with no pricing certainty or accountability

Breakdown events are high-stress, time-sensitive, and often occur in unsafe locations (highways, desert roads, parking structures). Customers lack real-time visibility into provider ETA, credentials, and final price before commitment.

Meanwhile, thousands of independent tow operators, mobile mechanics, battery specialists, and fuel delivery businesses operate with underutilized capacity and no digital demand channel.

---

## Solution

Salik Assist provides a mobile-first marketplace where customers:

1. Submit a service request with location, vehicle type, and issue category
2. Receive offers from the top matched nearby providers (target: top 3 within minutes)
3. Compare ETA, price estimate, ratings, and credentials
4. Accept an offer and track provider arrival in real time
5. Pay securely upon service completion and rate the experience

Providers receive qualified demand, route optimization, guaranteed payment flows, and reputation building—without surrendering ownership of their business.

---

## Market Opportunity

The GCC roadside assistance and vehicle recovery market is driven by:

- High vehicle ownership and urban sprawl
- Extreme climate impact on batteries and tires
- Tourism and expatriate populations unfamiliar with local providers
- Insurance and fleet operators seeking digitized dispatch partners

Serviceable addressable market (SAM) for digital marketplace transactions in UAE alone is estimated at **$180M–$240M annually** by Year 3, with KSA representing 3–4× UAE potential. See [Market Size](../02-Market-Research/Market-Size.md).

## Business Model

Salik Assist earns revenue through:

| Stream | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Take rate (commission)** | Percentage of completed job value (primary) |
| **Provider subscription** | Optional tiered plans for priority placement and analytics |
| **Surge / peak pricing share** | Platform share during high-demand windows |
| **B2B partnerships** | Insurers, fleets, rental companies, OEM roadside programs |
| **Promoted listings** | Paid visibility for verified providers in competitive zones |

The platform acts strictly as intermediary—providers set base rates within platform guardrails; customers contract service with the provider, facilitated by Salik Assist terms.

---

## Competitive Advantage

1. **Marketplace breadth:** Multi-category (tow, mechanic, battery, fuel, tire) vs. single-service competitors
2. **Transparent competitive offers:** Top-3 provider model reduces customer anxiety and wait time
3. **Verification rigor:** License, insurance, and background checks as trust moat
4. **GCC-native operations:** Arabic/English, local payment methods, highway corridor knowledge
5. **B2B2C channel:** Insurance and fleet integrations create recurring demand

## Go-To-Market Summary

**Phase 1 (MVP):** Dubai metro + key highway corridors; tow and battery as anchor services; 150–200 verified providers; consumer app launch with referral incentives.

**Phase 2:** Abu Dhabi, Sharjah; expand service categories; insurer pilot partnerships.

**Phase 3:** KSA Riyadh/Jeddah; B2B enterprise contracts; subscription products for frequent drivers.

Customer acquisition: performance marketing, SEO for emergency intent, partnerships with insurers and car dealerships, roadside signage QR codes, and app store presence.

Provider acquisition: field sales to tow yards, mechanic shops, battery retailers; zero commission launch period in launch zones.

## Business Examples

**Example 1 — Highway breakdown:** A motorist on E11 experiences a flat tire at 11 PM. Within 90 seconds, three verified tire/mobile mechanic providers respond with ETA (18 min, 22 min, 35 min) and price ranges. Customer selects the 18-minute provider, tracks arrival, pays AED 180 upon completion, and rates 5 stars.

**Example 2 — Insurance partnership:** An insurer embeds Salik Assist in its claims app. Policyholders tap "Roadside Help"; jobs are routed to Salik's network with pre-negotiated rates. Insurer pays platform fee per dispatch; customer sees zero out-of-pocket for covered events.

**Example 3 — Provider growth:** A small tow operator in Al Quoz joins Salik Assist, completes 40 jobs in month one, builds a 4.8 rating, and upgrades to a premium listing— increasing monthly revenue by 35% vs. phone-only dispatch.

## Business Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Marketplace-only model (no owned fleet) | Capital-efficient scale; leverages existing provider infrastructure; faster geographic expansion |
| Top-3 competitive offers vs. auto-assign | Reduces perceived unfairness; improves price transparency; increases provider competition and customer trust |
| UAE-first launch | Dense vehicle population, strong digital payments, regulatory clarity, manageable pilot geography |
| Commission-primary revenue | Aligns platform incentives with completed successful jobs; proven in mobility marketplaces |
| Verification before activation | Trust is the primary purchase driver in emergency contexts; unverified supply damages brand irreversibly |

## Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Provider supply shortage at launch | High | Pre-sign 150+ providers; launch incentives; geographic zone gating |
| Low trust in new brand during emergencies | High | Verification badges, insurer partnerships, money-back guarantee on disputes |
| Regulatory classification as transport operator | Medium | Legal review; marketplace terms; providers retain service liability |
| Incumbent insurer response | Medium | B2B partnership vs. competition positioning |
| Seasonality / demand spikes | Medium | Surge pricing rules, provider incentive bonuses |

Full register: [Risk Register](../04-Business/Risk-Register.md).

## Assumptions

- UAE motorists will adopt a new app for emergencies if ETA and price transparency are superior to calling unknown tow numbers
- Independent providers will accept 15–20% commission in exchange for demand and payment guarantee
- Average job value (tow + ancillary) of AED 200–350 in UAE metro
- 60% of requests occur within 20 km of provider base
- Insurance partnerships take 6–9 months to contract but yield 25%+ of volume at maturity
