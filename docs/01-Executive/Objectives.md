## Objective Framework

Objectives follow **SMART** criteria and map to four strategic themes:

| Theme | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **T1 — Network Density** | Sufficient verified supply and active demand in launch zones |
| **T2 — Trust & Quality** | SLAs, ratings, and dispute rates within targets |
| **T3 — Revenue & Unit Economics** | GMV growth with improving contribution margin |
| **T4 — Strategic Partnerships** | B2B channels driving recurring volume |

Each objective has a single accountable executive role (to be assigned at hiring).

## Objective Hierarchy

```
Vision (2031: default roadside infrastructure)
    └── Mission (trusted marketplace today)
            └── Strategic Objectives (Y1–Y3)
                    └── OKRs (quarterly)
                            └── KPIs (weekly/monthly dashboards)
```

Conflicts resolve upward: if a quarterly growth OKR threatens verification standards (mission), mission wins.

## Business Examples

**O1.4 in practice:** Operations monitors median time-to-first-offer hourly. If a zone exceeds 5 minutes for 3 consecutive days, launch team triggers provider recruitment sprint and temporary customer promotions paused until supply recovers.

**O2.5 in practice:** Fleet manager of 200-vehicle delivery company integrates Salik Assist; 400 jobs/month flow through B2B rate card—counting toward 20% B2B mix target.

**O3.1 in practice:** KSA launch delayed 2 months due to provider licensing complexity—executive team reprioritizes O3.3 job volume via deeper UAE penetration rather than accepting weak KSA liquidity.

## Business Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| Year 1 geographic focus on Dubai only | Concentrated liquidity beats national thin coverage |
| B2B objective deferred to LOI + pilot, not full contract | Realistic sales cycle; proof of consumer marketplace first |
| Dual-sided retention objectives | Marketplace fails if either side churns |
| Explicit dispute rate objective | Trust metric as important as volume |
