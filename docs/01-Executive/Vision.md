## Vision Statement

**To become the default digital infrastructure for roadside assistance across the Middle East and emerging markets—making vehicle breakdowns as manageable as ordering a ride or a meal.**

Every motorist, fleet operator, and insurer should think of Salik Assist first when a vehicle stops moving. Every qualified roadside professional should depend on the platform for fair demand, timely payment, and professional growth.

## Vision Narrative (Amazon PRFAQ Style)

### Press Release (Future Date: July 4, 2031)

**Salik Assist Now Powers 1 in 3 Roadside Rescues Across MENA**

*Dubai — Salik Assist*, the region's leading roadside assistance marketplace, today announced it has facilitated over 15 million rescue jobs across 12 countries since launch. The platform connects motorists to verified help in an average of 4 minutes—from towing and mobile repair to EV charging rescue and desert recovery.

Insurance partners including leading regional carriers now route 70% of motor claims roadside events through Salik Assist's API-less business integrations. Fleet operators report 40% reduction in vehicle downtime using Salik's SLA-backed dispatch.

"We set out to replace panic and phone calls with transparency and trust," said the CEO. "Roadside assistance is now a category consumers expect to work like Uber—fast, tracked, and fairly priced."

Salik Assist remains asset-light: 45,000 independent providers earn livelihoods through the platform while maintaining their businesses.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is Salik Assist a towing company?**  
A: No. Salik Assist is a marketplace. Providers remain independent businesses; the platform handles discovery, booking, payment, and quality standards.

**Q: Why will motorists switch from insurer hotlines?**  
A: Speed, choice, and visibility. Motorists see multiple offers, track arrival, and pay known prices—while insurers gain cost control and audit trails through B2B partnerships.

**Q: What markets next?**  
A: North Africa and South Asia expansion is underway, prioritizing countries with high motorization and fragmented roadside supply.

## Vision Pillars

### 1. Trust at the Moment of Need

Breakdowns are emotional. The vision prioritizes verified providers, transparent pricing, and accountability over raw speed or lowest cost alone.

### 2. Inclusive Provider Economy

The platform succeeds only when independent operators—tow drivers, mechanics, battery technicians—earn more and work more professionally through Salik Assist than without it.

### 3. Infrastructure, Not App

Long-term value is in becoming the dispatch layer insurers, fleets, governments, and navigation apps rely upon—not merely a consumer application.

### 4. Regional Depth Before Global Breadth

Win GCC completely before spreading thin. Local payment, language, regulation, and highway knowledge create defensibility global entrants struggle to replicate.

### 5. Asset-Light Scale

No owned fleets. Capital deploys toward network density, trust systems, and partnerships—not depreciating trucks.

## What We Will Not Become

- **A vertically integrated tow operator** competing with our supply side
- **A low-quality lead-gen directory** with unverified listings and hidden fees
- **An insurer** or warranty underwriter assuming mechanical liability
- **A general gig-economy platform** diluting focus into unrelated home services

## Business Examples

**2031 — Cross-border tourist:** A European tourist's rental car fails in Oman. Rental company's Salik Assist integration dispatches a verified bilingual provider within 8 minutes; charges settle between rental company and platform under existing B2B contract.

**2031 — EV highway corridor:** An EV runs low on charge between Abu Dhabi and Dubai. Salik Assist routes a mobile charging unit provider (category added Phase 3) with certified high-voltage handling credentials.

**2031 — Provider career path:** A provider starts as solo tow operator, grows to 6-truck fleet, uses Salik Assist analytics and premium tier to win corporate fleet contracts—all while remaining an independent business.

## Business Decisions

| Decision | Why It Supports Vision |
|----------|------------------------|
| Long-term marketplace positioning | Enables network effects and B2B infrastructure role |
| Investment in verification and SLAs | Trust is the vision's foundation; shortcuts undermine 2031 brand |
| Deferred owned-fleet experiments | Capital and brand alignment stay with supply partners |
| Arabic-first UX parity | Regional depth requires linguistic and cultural excellence |

## Risks

| Risk | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Vision–execution gap | Ambitious 2031 state requires sustained funding and operational excellence |
| Category creep | Expanding too fast into unrelated services dilutes "roadside" clarity |
| Platform disintermediation | Mature providers or insurers build direct relationships after introduction |
| Global competitor entry | Well-funded entrant bundles roadside into super-app |

## Assumptions

- MENA motorization and road infrastructure investment continue growing through 2031
- Digital payment and smartphone penetration remain high among target motorists
- Regulators continue permitting marketplace models with clear provider liability
- Climate and EV transition increase demand for diversified roadside categories
